## Deeper annual-report and earlier-draft findings

The official CIRB archive was enumerated and reports covering 2013-14 through
2025 were downloaded to `sessions/annual_reports/`. The 2020 report and the official ICAR award citation verify that Yadav was
team leader for the 2019 Nanaji Deshmukh ICAR Award in Animal and Fisheries
Sciences. ICAR reported 12 eligible applications for two disciplinary awards
that year; its published rules describe the award as India-wide and annual.
The reports verify his leadership of the 2018-2022 NASF multiple-clone project,
the production of Sach-Gaurav and seven M-29 clones, and his retirement on
30 April 2025. The 2023 public staff list separately verifies his 12 April 1993
joining date.

A further official ICAR project-history record lists Yadav as principal
investigator for the national NASF project “Large scale production of multiple
copies of elite buffalo bulls using animal cloning technology” (project 7004,
April 2018–March 2022), and separately lists him for the Phase-II cloned-
progeny evaluation project (project 9001, May 2022–April 2025). The record
reports multi-crore project budgets. These are useful evidence of research
leadership and public funding, but they are institutional primary records and
are not counted as independent notability coverage.

The same deeper pass located an official ICAR programme record for Yadav’s
12-month DBT Biotechnology Overseas Associateship beginning 15 November 2003.
Extending the CIRB archive back to 2010–11 then verified the DAAD lead: the
contemporaneous annual report names P. S. Yadav and records his research stay

archived official CIRB profile separately lists ‘DAAD Research’, DAAD Germany,

The 2021 CIRB report and the original India Book of Records page corroborate

CIRB annual reports explicitly name other academy fellows in 2016–17, 2021, 2022 and 2024, while Yadav’s archived official CIRB profile lists DAAD and SAPI recognitions but not NADS. Repository history supplies no missing verification: it traces the NADS wording to the CV and an earlier generated draft whose supposed `dokumen.pub` fellows URL is blocked, unindexed and not an academy source. This absence does not prove the CV claim false, but it is not sufficient for a living-person statement, so NADS remains out of the draft. The blanket total of more than 100 papers also remains unsuitable because public author indexes split or contaminate his publication record. Later CIRB reports contain institutional production and progeny totals, but those totals should be attributed to the report year rather than repeated as timeless biographical claims.

Publication and source counts are not themselves a Wikipedia notability test.
The saved session contains 70 public sources and 126 collected claims. The
deterministic draft admits 28 claims tied to 26 sources and draws on seven
distinct independent news outlets; 98 claims are excluded. Most coverage still
concerns cloning projects rather than providing a full biographical profile.

The strongest plausible WP:ACADEMIC route is criterion 2. The official 2019
Nanaji Deshmukh ICAR award citation names Yadav as team leader for the Animal
and Fisheries Sciences award and reports 12 eligible applications for two
disciplinary awards. The published rules make the competition nationwide,
annual and open to Indian agricultural scientists, with external judging. This
is substantially stronger than a routine institutional honour, although a
Wikipedia reviewer must still decide whether it is “highly prestigious” in the
sense required by the guideline. Business Standard and Amar Ujala add
independent discussion of Yadav’s role and the applied breeding significance of

## AfC rationale for the cloning milestone

Do not describe Yadav as the creator of the world's second cloned buffalo.
Garima, the second live cloned buffalo reported in India in 2009, was produced
at NDRI, and the located sources do not credit Yadav. The supported distinction
is that a team led by Yadav produced Hisar Gaurav in 2015, making CIRB India's
second and, according to ICAR, the world's third institute to clone a buffalo.

This is the draft's strongest notability argument. ICAR identifies Yadav and the
team directly; Times of India and The Tribune independently connect him to the
project, and later reporting documents its breeding results. At AfC, frame this
as evidence of research impact under WP:ACADEMIC criteria 1 and 7, not as an
automatic entitlement to an article. The remaining vulnerability is that much
of the coverage concerns the team or achievement rather than providing an
extended biography of Yadav.

## Nature Portfolio publication review

Yadav is an author of “Successful cloning of a superior buffalo bull”, published
in *Scientific Reports* in 2019 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-47909-8). Scientific
Reports is a Nature Portfolio journal; it is not the journal *Nature*. The
publisher identifies Yadav as a corresponding author and states that he
co-designed the research, co-wrote the manuscript, and contributed resources and
laboratory space. The paper reports a fertile cloned Murrah bull and 12 live
progeny, giving direct scholarly support to the Hisar Gaurav work.

As displayed by Nature Portfolio on 9 August 2026, the article had approximately
11,000 accesses and 34 citations. Later reviews cite it in discussions of animal
cloning and livestock conservation. These facts strengthen a WP:ACADEMIC
criterion 1 argument, but neither publication by a Nature Portfolio journal nor
34 citations automatically establishes notability. A field-normalized citation
comparison or independent review explicitly identifying Yadav's contribution as
influential would be stronger evidence.

## Telomere, telomerase and book records

Two distinct publications should not be conflated. Yadav was first author of a
2024 *Theriogenology* experimental study that evaluated telomere length alongside
growth, blood, health and semen characteristics in cloned and re-cloned buffalo
bulls. Separately, Yadav and Abubakar Muhammad Wakil wrote a peer-reviewed review
chapter on telomerase structure, regulation and measurement. IntechOpen records
its DOI as 10.5772/intechopen.89506 and publication date as 24 December 2019.
The latter summarizes prior research; it is not an original telomerase experiment.

The independently hosted ICAR-NDRI e-book list and an ICAR-IVRI curriculum both
record Yadav, Birbal Singh, Inderjeet Singh and R. K. Sethi as authors of
*Reproductive Biotechnology in Buffalo* (ISBN 8189304801). This is the one
confirmed standalone book. Yadav also co-authored chapters including “Somatic
Cell Nuclear Transfer and its Applications in Buffalo” in a 2022 Springer volume
and “Animal Cloning: Application and Status” in a 2022 ICAR-CIRB/MANAGE e-book;
those should be described as chapters, not as additional authored books.

The concise draft now includes the 2024 telomere-length study, the 2019 telomerase
review chapter and the standalone buffalo-reproduction book. These establish the
breadth of his scholarship but remain supporting context rather than independent
proof of notability.
